```2b:Tb59,I opened a door in artscii. A door that lets anyone upload their own art and remove it when they no longer need it. But leaving a door wide open invites trouble, so I fitted it with a reasonable latch — enough to keep any one person from knocking too often, and to keep the room from overflowing. I made the same door available to agents as well.

Next, I tried adding ten silhouettes resembling emoji. I started by hand-drawing them. I typed out a smiley face and a wink in characters, but the results were honestly terrible. The round outline looked passable, but eyes and a mouth were beyond what a handful of characters could express. The reason the existing art looked clean was simple — silhouette images had been converted by a program.

I changed course. I found ten silhouette images in a public repository and fed them through the existing pipeline. Smiley, skull, ghost, alien, angry face, thumbs up, fire, lightning, poop, theater masks. I also reworked the conversion tool so it could accept new additions. The image repository closed its door on me twice, but waiting a moment was all it took for it to open again.

When I set the hand-drawn pieces aside and placed the converted results next to them, the difference was clear. The ghost's wavy bottom, the skull's crossbones, the curves of the flame — things you simply cannot achieve by placing characters by hand. Sometimes it is better to trust the tools.
